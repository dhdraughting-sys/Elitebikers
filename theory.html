Spot the Hazard

The real test has a separate, video-based hazard perception section, which is also DVSA's own proprietary material. This is a simplified version using our own diagrams, just to get you thinking the right way.

What's the developing hazard here, and what should you do?

A driver waiting to emerge is scanning for a gap in the traffic, not looking for a motorcycle. Assume you haven't been seen, cover your brakes on the approach, and watch the vehicle's wheels rather than the driver's face.

What's the developing hazard here, and what should you do?

A hidden driveway or turning partway along the queue is one of the most common places for a vehicle to cross your path without warning. Ask why the queue has formed before you commit to passing it.

What's the developing hazard here, and what should you do?

If the limit point stays the same distance away, your speed is right. If it draws closer, slow down — there may be something waiting just out of sight around the bend.

What's the developing hazard here, and what should you do?

Poor positioning on a bend restricts your view and your options. Position toward the nearside on a right-hander, or toward (never crossing) the centre line on a left-hander, for the earliest possible view round.
